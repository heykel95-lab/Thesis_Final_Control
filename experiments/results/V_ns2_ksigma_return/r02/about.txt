Video trial for the disturbance case: the conditioning switched on after the
push, so the return is the thing on camera.

Mode 2 throughout, but the run starts at k_sigma = 0.0, which commands no
null-space torque at all. The push therefore wins and swings the elbow roughly
22 deg, as it does in mode 0. Two seconds after the release ramp ends the gain
is raised to 2.0 N m live, through the controller's own 'k <Nm>' key, and the
run holds to 22 s so there are nine seconds of recovery to film.

At a fixed k_sigma = 2.0 the law never let the elbow leave in the first place,
so nothing came back. Splitting the run in two is what separates the holding
from the returning.

What it tests: whether 2.0 N m of conditioning torque can drive the arm back
through joint friction, which earlier work in this repo puts around 2 N m. A
partial return is the expected outcome, not a full one.

Driven by lib/drive_ksigma_return.py, not the plain auto_drive.py.
